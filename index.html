Stability and Deception in Games on Manifolds

Mahmoud Abdelgalil · Miroslav Krstic · Jorge I. Poveda

University at Buffalo · University of California San Diego

We study generalized equilibrium seeking in multi-agent systems whose joint state evolves on a manifold and whose agents act through state-dependent, potentially non-holonomic control vector fields. Under a bundle-splitting condition, we show that the averaged interaction dynamics admit an intrinsic representation as a sum of projected Riemannian gradients, giving rise to a form of non-holonomic gradient play. We characterize the local stability of equilibria through an intrinsic linearization that explicitly captures the roles of the Riemannian connection and the non-holonomy of the actuation frame. We then develop a geometric theory of deception, in which a malicious agent exploits privileged information about other agents’ learning rules to shift the equilibrium of the interaction. We show that deception effectively tilts the Riemannian geometry perceived by the victim agents, thereby distorting their projected gradient directions. Exponential stability persists over an open set of deception parameters, and the resulting equilibrium displacement admits an explicit first-order characterization.

00 · PROBLEM SETUP

Many agents. One geometric state.

The agents do not evolve as independent points. They act on the same state \(x\in\mathcal M\) through different, state-dependent directions. Each payoff \(J_i(x)\) depends on that common state, so the game is coupled both through the dynamics and through the objectives.

CORE COUPLING

Agent \(i\) injects scalar inputs into its own actuation fields \(f_{i,j}(x)\in T_x\mathcal M\), while observing only its payoff \(J_i(x)\).

01 · THE MAIN OBJECT

Gradient play, without pretending the world is flat.

A shared state \(x\in\mathcal M\) evolves on a smooth manifold. Agent \(i\) maximizes its payoff \(J_i\), but it can act only through a family of vector fields \(f_{i,j}\). The resulting interaction is

\[ \dot{x} = \sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_i \sum_{j=1}^{d_i} \mathcal{L}_{f_{i,j}}(J_i)(x)\,f_{i,j}(x). \tag{3} \]

This is the central object of the paper: payoff ascent filtered through the geometry of what each agent can physically actuate.

02 · GEOMETRY

What an agent wants and what it can do are different objects.

Under a bundle-splitting condition, the paper constructs a Riemannian metric for which the interaction admits the intrinsic representation \[ \dot x=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_i\,\operatorname{grad}_{E_i}J_i(x). \]

03 · LOCAL STABILITY

The linearization remembers the curvature of the actuation frame.

At an equilibrium \(x^\star\), stability is governed by the intrinsic Jacobian \(A|_{x^\star}\). Its blocks split into two conceptually different pieces.

Isolated equilibrium if \(A|_{x^\star}\) is an automorphism.

Locally exponentially stable iff \(A|_{x^\star}\) is Hurwitz.

\[ (A_{ij})_{p,q} = \underbrace{\operatorname{Hess}(J_i)[f_{i,p},f_{j,q}]}_{\text{payoff curvature}} + \underbrace{dJ_i[\nabla_{f_{j,q}}f_{i,p}]}_{\text{connection / non-holonomy}}. \tag{20} \]
Hessian termHow the payoff bends.
Connection termHow the admissible directions bend.

The second term has no analogue in ordinary coordinate-wise gradient play and is the geometric signature of a moving, potentially non-commuting frame.

04 · GEOMETRIC DECEPTION

The deceiver does not alter the victim's payoff.
It tilts the directions through which that payoff acts.

With privileged knowledge of the victim's learning signal, agent 1 can produce \[ \widetilde e_1=e_1,\qquad \widetilde e_2=e_2+\gamma e_1. \] The victim's projected gradient is therefore biased, and the equilibrium moves.

THEOREM 3

Stable equilibria persist under sufficiently small deception.

If \(x^\star\) is locally exponentially stable for the nominal interaction, then there exists an open neighborhood of deception parameters and a smooth map \(X^\star(\gamma)\) with \(X^\star(0)=x^\star\), such that the shifted equilibrium remains locally exponentially stable.

FIRST-ORDER EQUILIBRIUM SHIFT
\[ \left. \frac{\partial X^\star}{\partial \gamma_j^k} \right|_{\gamma=0} = -dJ_2[f_{1,k}](x^\star)\, (A|_{x^\star})^{-1}f_{2,j}(x^\star). \tag{28} \]

06 · MODEL-FREE CONNECTION

The same geometry is the target behavior of payoff-only learning.

Lie-bracket-based feedback laws can use measurements of \(J_i\) only and approximate the non-holonomic gradient-play dynamics at high probing frequencies.

PAYOFF-ONLY IMPLEMENTATION · EQ. (4) \[ u_{i,j}(x,t)= \sqrt{4\pi\kappa_i\omega_{i,j}\omega}\, \cos(2\pi\omega_{i,j}\omega t)\, F^{1}_{i,j}\!\left(J_i(x)\right) - \sqrt{4\pi\kappa_i\omega_{i,j}\omega}\, \sin(2\pi\omega_{i,j}\omega t)\, F^{2}_{i,j}\!\left(J_i(x)\right). \tag{4} \]

Only measurements of \(J_i(x)\) are required. As \(\omega\to\infty\), the associated Lie-bracket approximation coincides with the non-holonomic gradient-play dynamics in (3).
